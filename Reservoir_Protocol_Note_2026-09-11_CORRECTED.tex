\section{Part 0 objects that this paper uses}\label{sec:objects}

Three objects in Part 0 must be kept distinct.\cite{part0}

\subsection{Object A: local GKSL container}

Let $\mathcal{H}_{AB} = \mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B$ be finite-dimensional, associated with
spacelike-separated regions $R_A$, $R_B$. The joint generator is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_E[\rho] = -i[ H_A + H_B, \rho] + \sum_{\alpha\in\{A,B\}}\sum_k \gamma_k\big( E(x_\alpha) \big)\, \mathcal{D}\big[ M_k^{(\alpha)} \big] \rho,
\label{eq:lindblad}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}[L]\rho = L\rho L^\dagger - \tfrac{1}{2}\{L^\dagger L, \rho\},
\label{eq:diss}
\end{equation}
each $M_k^{(\alpha)}$ supported only on party $\alpha$, and $\gamma_k: \mathbb{R}\to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ measurable.
For frozen $E(x)$ and nonnegative rates, \eqref{eq:lindblad} is standard GKSL and generates a
completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) semigroup.\cite{Lindblad, Gorini, Breuer}

The field equation for the modulator is hyperbolic,
\begin{equation}
(\Box + m_E^2)\, E(x) = J[\Psi](x),
\label{eq:field}
\end{equation}
with $J$ a prescribed local classical source or a specified semiclassical expectation of local matter
operators. A consistent quantum--classical backreaction prescription is additional model input.
Retarded boundary conditions make $E(x)$ depend only on sources in the causal past of $x$.
Part 0 distinguishes an ultralight hidden scalar benchmark $m_\Phi \sim 10^{-4}$--$10^{-3}$ eV from a
separate Higgs-facing state if that sector is included, and uses $\Lambda_\star = 1$ TeV only as an
EFT cutoff benchmark---not as an infrared bath cutoff.

\subsection{Object B: sufficient no-signaling}

\begin{theorem}[Part 0, Theorem 1.1; sufficient operational no-signaling]\label{thm:nosig}
Consider choices $a, a'$ made in $R_A$. While $R_A$ and $R_B$ are spacelike, suppose
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}^{(a)}(t) = \mathcal{L}^{(a)}_A(t)\otimes \mathrm{id}_B + \mathrm{id}_A \otimes \mathcal{L}^{(b)}_B(t)
\label{eq:factor}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{L}_B^{(b)}(t)$ independent of $a$. Then Bob's reduced state is independent of Alice's choice.
\end{theorem}

The result is sufficient, not a classification of all no-signaling open dynamics.\cite{Gisin1,Polchinski}
A deterministic nonlinear reading of the scalar sector is outside the theorem and would need a
separate causality analysis.

\subsection{Object C: exponential as composition, not as Gleason}

\begin{assumption}[Part 0, Chapter 3]\label{asm:comp}
A local GKSL channel with unperturbed rate $\gamma_k^0 > 0$ is rescaled by a classical functional $E_k$,
\begin{equation}
\gamma_k(E_k) = \gamma_k^0\, h_k(E_k), \qquad h_k: \mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}.
\label{eq:hk}
\end{equation}
$E_k$ is a local field value or a smeared $c$-number functional fixed by the apparatus. It is not an
eigenvalue of an operator on the system Hilbert space. For each channel $k$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Normalization: $h_k(0)=1$.
\item Positivity: $h_k(E) > 0$ on the EFT domain.
\item Continuity at $E=0$.
\item Independent additive composition:
\begin{equation}
h_k(E_1 + E_2) = h_k(E_1)\, h_k(E_2).
\label{eq:cauchy}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Part 0, Theorem 3.1]\label{thm:exp}
Any $h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ satisfying Assumption~\ref{asm:comp} is $h(E) = e^{\eta E}$ for some $\eta\in\mathbb{R}$.
\end{theorem}

Equation \eqref{eq:cauchy} is the load-bearing model choice. It is appropriate when independent
source contributions affect the same local rate without extra cross-couplings. It is \emph{not} implied by
complete positivity and does not follow from Gleason's theorem. Equation \eqref{eq:hk} changes the
timescale of a dissipative channel. It does not introduce a nonlinear state-dependent probability
rule. One-shot outcome weights remain those of the density operator and the measurement
operators (CORE-03).

\section{What a reservoir derivation is required to be}\label{sec:requirements}

A reservoir derivation is a chain with no interpretive step between links.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Named system $S$ whose clicks or contrast are counted.
\item Named environment $R$: modes, temperature, spectral density, coupling operator.
\item Named classical background $E(x)$ that is \emph{not} an operator on $\mathcal{H}_S$. It may enter the
system--bath Hamiltonian, bath frequencies, or a gate on the coupling.
\item A controlled approximation (Born--Markov, weak coupling, secular, or a monitored
unraveling limit) that produces a GKSL generator on $S$.
\item Proof that surviving $E$-dependence sits in rates $\gamma_k(E)$, not in a nonlinear map $\rho \mapsto P_i(\rho,E)$.
\item Proof that $E$ at a point $x_B$ is a retarded functional of sources in $J^-(x_B)$ only, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:nosig} still applies.
\item An expansion that either derives $h(E)=e^{\eta E}$ from a composition property of that bath,
or replaces it with the law the bath actually gives.
\end{enumerate}
Three places $E$ can sit in a microscopic Hamiltonian. Only one is the Part 0 mechanism.

\textbf{Place 1: coherent Hamiltonian on $S$.} $H_S(E) = H_S + gE\sigma_z$ is a Stark shift. It changes
energies and interferometer phase. It does not by itself change a GKSL rate. Allowed as an extra
term. Not the rate mechanism.

\textbf{Place 2: bath frequencies or occupation.} $\omega_q(E)$ or $T=T(E)$ can change Golden-Rule
rates. Danger: if $E$ is sourced by distant matter, Bob's bath must not hear Alice's setting
spacelike. Retarded Green function for $E$, or Theorem~\ref{thm:nosig} does not cover the experiment.

\textbf{Place 3: coupling gate (the slot used below).} The bath sees a system operator multiplied
by a dimensionless gate $\lambda(E)$ with $\lambda(0)=1$. In Born--Markov the jump operators stay the
same operators; only rates pick up $\lambda(E)^2$.

\label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
with, during the monitored arms (beamsplitters already applied),

\section{Born--Markov generator and visibility}\label{sec:bornmarkov}

Assume the bath is in a thermal state $\tau_R$ at inverse temperature $\beta$, $\langle B\rangle_{\tau_R} = 0$,
coupling is weak, and bath memory is short compared with system and rate scales
(Section~\ref{sec:ineq}, inequality I). The two-time correlator is
\begin{equation}
C(t) = \mathrm{Tr}_R\, B(t)B(0)\,\tau_R = \int_0^\infty \!\! d\omega\, J(\omega) \Big[ \big( n(\omega)+1 \big) e^{-i\omega t} + n(\omega) e^{i\omega t} \Big],
\label{eq:C}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
n(\omega) = \frac{1}{e^{\beta\omega} - 1}.
\label{eq:n}
\end{equation}
Because $S = \LR$ commutes with $H_S$, the coupling is exactly dephasing. The reduced generator is
\begin{equation}
\dot\rho = \mathcal{L}_E \rho = -i\big[ H_S + \Delta(E)\LR, \rho \big] + \Gamma_\phi(E)\, \mathcal{D}\big[ \LR \big] \rho.
\label{eq:gen}
\end{equation}
The Lamb shift $\Delta(E)$ is the principal-value piece times $\lambda(\Delta E)^2$. It is Place 1 riding
along; it is a path-dependent phase and does not replace the rate story. The dissipator coefficient is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_\phi(E) = \lambda(\Delta E)^2\, \Gamma_\phi^{(0)}, \qquad
\Gamma_\phi^{(0)} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dt\, C(t) = 2\pi \lim_{\omega\to 0^+} J(\omega) \coth\!\Big( \frac{\beta\omega}{2}\Big).
\label{eq:gamma0}
\end{equation}

For an Ohmic bath $J(\omega) = \eta_{\mathrm{bath}}\, \omega\, e^{-\omega/\omega_c}$ at $k_B T \ll \omega_c$,
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_\phi^{(0)} = 4\pi\, \eta_{\mathrm{bath}}\, k_B T.
\label{eq:ohmic}
\end{equation}

\begin{correction}
\noindent\textbf{Correction note (factor of two reconciled).}
The printed note gave \eqref{eq:ohmic} as $\Gamma_\phi^{(0)} = 2\pi\, \eta_{\mathrm{bath}} k_B T$.
Substituting the stated Ohmic spectrum $J(\omega) = \eta_{\mathrm{bath}}\,\omega\,e^{-\omega/\omega_c}$
into the printed \eqref{eq:gamma0} (with $\coth(\beta\omega/2) \to 2/\beta\omega$ at $\omega\to0^+$:
$\;J(\omega)\coth(\beta\omega/2) \to 2\eta_{\mathrm{bath}}k_B T$, then multiplied by the printed $2\pi$) gives
$4\pi\,\eta_{\mathrm{bath}} k_B T$---a factor of two above the printed \eqref{eq:ohmic}. The two printed
equations were mutually inconsistent under the stated spectral convention.
\medskip\par\noindent
\textbf{Repair.} The dissipator coefficient is fixed by the printed conventions of \eqref{eq:C}--\eqref{eq:n}
(correlator normalization) and \eqref{eq:gamma0} (integral prefactor). Under those conventions the
Ohmic limit is $4\pi\,\eta_{\mathrm{bath}} k_B T$, and \eqref{eq:ohmic} is corrected to that value. The
coherence decay rate in \eqref{eq:rhodot} is $\tfrac12 \Gamma_\phi^{(0)} = 2\pi\,\eta_{\mathrm{bath}} k_B T$; the
factor of $\tfrac12$ there is the standard Hermitian-coupling dephasing relation
($\mathcal{D}[\LR]$ gives $\tfrac12$ on the off-diagonal), \emph{not} a factor lost in the spectrum. The
allowed methods sentence (Appendix~\ref{app:methods}) is updated to ``$4\pi\,\eta_{\mathrm{bath}} k_BT$'' to
stay in the same convention. Any future derivation that adopts the \emph{symmetrized}-correlator
convention must state so explicitly and will carry the corresponding $\tfrac12$ internally; mixing the
two conventions is exactly the failure this correction removes.
\end{correction}

At strictly zero temperature the DC Ohmic noise vanishes and a finite $\Gamma_\phi$ must not be quoted
without a stated infrared theory. Room-temperature monitors are not that limit.

\textbf{Contrast.} For an equal superposition, $\rho_{LR}(0) = 1/2$,
\begin{equation}
\dot\rho_{LR} = -\Big( i\big( \omega_L - \omega_R + \Delta(E) \big) + \tfrac12 \Gamma_\phi(E) \Big) \rho_{LR}.
\label{eq:rhodot}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Extra contrast against a baseline} $V_{\mathrm{QM}}$. Define, once, the two reference settings the

\item \textbf{Monitor-on, $E=0$ reference}: coupler active, $\lambda(0)=1$. Call it $V_{\mathrm{QM}}^{(\mathrm{on})}$.
\item \textbf{Monitor-off reference}: coupler physically decoupled ($\lambda$ inactive). Call it $V_{\mathrm{QM}}^{(\mathrm{off})}$.
\end{itemize}
The standard form printed below subtracts the $E=0$ piece of $\Gamma_\phi$ and therefore refers to the

\label{eq:contrast}

\alpha_{\mathrm{on}}(E) \equiv -\ln\!\Big( V / V_{\mathrm{QM}}^{(\mathrm{on})} \Big) = \tfrac12 \Gamma_\phi^{(0)}T \big( \lambda(\Delta E)^2 - 1 \big).
\label{eq:extra}

\begin{correction}
\noindent\textbf{Correction note (baseline made explicit).}
The printed note's \eqref{eq:contrast} subtracted the monitor's $E=0$ contribution
(the ``$-1$'') ``if desired,'' while Section~9 permitted a \emph{monitor-off} baseline from row N0. Those

\section{When the exponential is legal}\label{sec:whenexp}

From either twin,
\begin{equation}
h(\Delta E) = \lambda(\Delta E)^2.
\label{eq:hlambda}
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:exp} applies to $h$ if and only if $\lambda$ itself composes,
\begin{equation}
\lambda(\Delta E_1 + \Delta E_2) = \lambda(\Delta E_1)\,\lambda(\Delta E_2), \qquad \lambda(0) = 1, \quad \lambda > 0 \text{ continuous},
\label{eq:lambdacomp}
\end{equation}
hence $\lambda(\Delta E) = e^{\eta\Delta E/2}$ and $h(\Delta E) = e^{\eta\Delta E}$. That is an assumption on
the gate, not a gift of the Ohmic bath. The bath supplied the square. The exponential is the
statement that the microscopic coupling is multiplicative in $E$ (dilaton-like), or that two independent
weak gates multiply.

If the axiom is refused, the local chart is
\begin{equation}
\lambda(\Delta E) = 1 + \tfrac12 \eta \Delta E + O\big((\Delta E)^2\big) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h(\Delta E) = 1 + \eta \Delta E + O\big((\Delta E)^2\big),
\label{eq:taylor}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\frac{V}{V_{\mathrm{QM}}^{(\mathrm{on})}} = \exp\!\Big[ -\tfrac12 \Gamma_\phi^{(0)} T \Big( \eta\,\Delta E + O\!\big( (\Delta E)^2,\, T\eta^2(\Delta E)^2 \big) \Big) \Big].
\label{eq:laysmall}
\end{equation}
A methods section that prints $e^{\eta E}$ without saying which of those two it means is a Taylor series
in liturgical type.

\section{Three inequalities}\label{sec:ineq}

Print these above any exponential, or do not print the exponential.

\begin{proposition}[Markov / weak coupling]\label{prop:markov}
Let $\tau_B = \max(\omega_c^{-1}, \hbar/k_B T)$ be the bath memory, $\Gamma = \Gamma_\phi(E)$ (or
$\gamma(E)$ in the jump twin) the induced rate, and $\Omega$ the relevant Bohr gap. Then
\begin{equation}
\Gamma\, \tau_B \ll 1, \qquad |\lambda(\Delta E)\, g|/\omega_c \ll 1.
\label{eq:markovcond}
\end{equation}
If $E(t)$ moves, add
\begin{equation}
\tau_B\, |\dot E / E| \ll 1
\label{eq:slow}
\end{equation}
on the monitor support (equivalently: freeze $E$ during each scattering).
\end{proposition}

Without Proposition~\ref{prop:markov} there is no GKSL container and Theorem~\ref{thm:nosig} has
nothing to hold.

\begin{proposition}[Domain of the gate law]\label{prop:gate}
Either $\lambda(\Delta E) = e^{\eta\Delta E/2}$ on the whole EFT interval quoted, or $h(\Delta E)$ is
published from the actual $\lambda$ and the phrase ``exponential rate law'' is not used.
In addition, $|\eta\,\Delta E|$ must be small enough that neglected EFT operators
$O\!\big((\Delta E)^2/\Lambda_\star^2\big)$ remain smaller than the kept gate. The benchmark
$\Lambda_\star = 1$ TeV is not the infrared cutoff $\omega_c$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Locality / no-signaling]\label{prop:locality}
The modes $\{b_q\}$ are supported in a neighborhood of $x_L$ whose correlation length $\ell_c \sim c\tau_B$
satisfies $\ell_c \ll \Delta x$ and $\ell_c \ll d_{AB}$ to any spacelike partner. $E(x)$ is computed with the
retarded propagator only. No shared reservoir with the other path or with a spacelike partner. The
source $J$ is a local classical density or a local semiclassical $\langle O(x)\rangle$, not a functional of
the global interferometer coherence.
\end{proposition}

\textbf{Three leaks (failure modes of Theorem~\ref{thm:nosig} in reservoir language).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Common bath.} Alice and Bob couple to the same modes. Then $\mathcal{L}$ is not a sum of
local pieces.\cite{Gisin1,Polchinski}
\item \textbf{Quantized fast $E$.} If Alice's jump dumps a quantum into $E$ that Bob's bath absorbs
spacelike, a messenger has been built. Keep $E$ classical on the measurement timescale with
retarded sourcing.
\item \textbf{Non-local $J[\rho]$.} $J = \kappa_E S^2$ is local if $S$ is a local operator density. Replacing it
by a coherence functional of the whole laboratory wavefunction kills factorization. That replacement
is how interpretive papers re-enter the generator. It is not used here.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Estimator}\label{sec:estimator}

Detected quanta $N$ after the second beamsplitter, plus a dark-port or swapped-phase channel.
For a two-port Mach--Zehnder scanned in phase $\phi$,
\begin{equation}
N_\pm(\phi) = \tfrac12 N_{\mathrm{tot}} \big( 1 \pm V\cos\phi \big).
\label{eq:ports}
\end{equation}
Let $\hat{V}$ be the fit (or a four-phase stepping estimator). Baseline $\hat{V}_{\mathrm{QM}} ^{(\mathrm{on})}$ from
contemporaneous N1 (monitor-on, $\Delta E = 0$; or from N1 after N1 is shown dead), unless the
analysis explicitly adopts the monitor-off baseline $\hat{V}_{\mathrm{QM}}^{(\mathrm{off})}$ from N0. Then
\begin{equation}
\hat\alpha = -\ln\Big( \hat{V} / \hat{V}_{\mathrm{QM}} \Big), \qquad
\text{(baseline declared: on or off)}.
\label{eq:alphaest}
\end{equation}

\textbf{N0 used as a monitor-off baseline.} Then $\hat\alpha_{\mathrm{off}}$ measures
$\tfrac12 \Gamma_\phi^{(0)}T\,\lambda(\Delta E)^2$; because $\lambda(0)=1$, N0 itself returns
$\hat\alpha_{\mathrm{off}}(\mathrm{N0}) \to 0$ only if the $E{=}0$ dephasing contribution
$\tfrac12 \Gamma_\phi^{(0)}T$ is separately known to vanish---it generally does not. The corrected
convention therefore defaults to the monitor-on reference, in which N0 remains a coupler-off
``offset meter'' (its \emph{dip} diagnostic checks that the monitor contributes the expected
$\tfrac12 \Gamma_\phi^{(0)}T$ offset), while N1 is the $\alpha=0$ null by construction.

Near-optimal stepping and $V$ not pathological give the shot-noise sketch
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Var}(\hat V) \simeq \frac{1 - V^2}{N_{\mathrm{tot}}}, \qquad
\sigma_\alpha \simeq \frac{\sigma_V}{V}.
\label{eq:noise}
\end{equation}
Near $V \approx V_{\mathrm{QM}} \approx 1$ and $\alpha \ll 1$, $\sigma_\alpha \simeq N_{\mathrm{tot}}^{-1/2}$.
Dirty fringes replace this line with the actual fit covariance.

\subsection{Power inequality}

Linearize N2 for small $x = \eta\,\Delta E$:
\begin{equation}
\alpha \simeq \tfrac12 \Gamma_\phi^{(0)} T\, \eta\,\Delta E.
\label{eq:lin}
\end{equation}
Rejecting $\alpha = 0$ at significance $z$ requires $\alpha \ge z\sigma_\alpha$, hence
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{tot}} \ge \frac{4z^2}{\big( \Gamma_\phi^{(0)} T\, \eta\,\Delta E \big)^2}
\label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
in the shot-noise idealization. Worked illustration only: $\Gamma_\phi^{(0)} = 10^{3}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$,
$T = 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}$, $|\eta\,\Delta E| \sim 10^{-2}$ gives $\alpha \sim 5\times10^{-4}$ and
$N_{\mathrm{tot}} \gtrsim 4\times10^{8}$ for $z=5$. If $\Gamma_\phi^{(0)}$, $T$, and independently metered
$\Delta E$ cannot be stated before opening N2, there is no power analysis.

\textbf{Nuisance terms that must sit inside $\sigma_\alpha$ or the count is fictitious:} source drift between N0
and N2; pointing and overlap; coupler-on thermal load (N3 exists to catch this); path-length wander
mistaken for contrast loss (always scan $\phi$); actuator systematics (coil Zeeman, orientation
wander); post-hoc cuts. The budget inequality is
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\alpha,\mathrm{sys}}^2 + \sigma_{\alpha,\mathrm{stat}}^2 \le \big( \alpha_{\min}/ z \big)^2,
\label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_{\min}$ the smallest Place-3 signal claimed to matter, and the left-hand side taken from
measured N0/N1 residuals.

\section{Conclusions}

rates without touching one-shot Born weights, and a sufficient no-signaling theorem follows
if the generator factorizes with retarded $c$-number coefficients. The exponential rate law is a
Cauchy-equation corollary of a composition axiom, not a fact about reservoirs.

This note wrote the missing middle for one device. An Ohmic thermal pointer bath coupled
through $\lambda(\Delta E)\,\LR$ produces $\Gamma_\phi(E) = \lambda(\Delta E)^2 \Gamma_\phi^{(0)}$ (with

exponential appears if and only if the gate composes. Three inequalities decide whether that
sentence may enter a methods section. A four-row protocol exists that can say no to Place-3
